Flat-fee vs usage pricing: read the bill before it reads you.

Opinion · 4 min readBy the HourKeep team

If you can't compute next month's bill on a napkin, you don't have pricing — you have a surprise schedule.

Usage-based pricing isn't evil. It makes perfect sense for things with real marginal costs — compute, bandwidth, API calls. The problem starts when vendors meter things that cost them nothing: your projects, your clients, your invoices. Then "usage" isn't cost recovery. It's a claim on your growth. Here are five questions we ask about every tool before it touches our stack.

1. Can I compute next month's bill on a napkin?

The napkin test is the whole game. Seats × rate = bill? Pass. Seats × rate + "platform activity units" defined in a 40-page metering guide? Fail. If the vendor's own sales rep can't quote your renewal to the dollar, neither can you — and finance teams can't budget a shrug.

2. Does my success increase my bill?

This is the consultancy trap. You win two new clients, open six new projects, send forty more invoices — and your time-tracking bill climbs, even though none of that costs the vendor an extra cent. A tool should get cheaper per unit of value as you grow, not more expensive. If growth punishes you, the pricing is backwards.

3. Are the meters published or "contact sales"?

Published meters can be judged. Unpublished meters — "custom usage tiers," "enterprise activity bands," fees that appear at renewal — cannot. Treat any meter you can't read before signing as a blank cheque, because functionally that's what it is. The Harvest bills that made the BBC all shared one feature: the fees were unpublished until the invoice.

If growth punishes you, the pricing is backwards. Full stop.

4. What happens to my price if they're acquired?

Ask it directly, on the sales call, and watch the reaction. Acquisitions reprice — new owners buy revenue, then grow it. The only defence is a written grandfather clause: your rate locked for as long as you stay, surviving ownership changes. A vendor confident in their pricing will put that in the contract. A vendor planning the 16× playbook will change the subject.

5. Can I leave with everything, for free, today?

Full CSV + JSON export, self-serve, no support ticket, no "data extraction fee." Try it during the trial, before you're dependent. Exportable data does two jobs: it proves the vendor's confidence, and it caps your worst case. The day leaving becomes hard, staying stops being a decision — and vendors price hostages differently than customers.

The two lines that matter

Strip every SaaS contract down to two lines and most pricing anxiety disappears:

  • A number that can't move: flat monthly fee, in writing, grandfathered for existing customers if pricing ever changes.
  • An exit that can't close: free, complete, self-serve export of everything — projects, clients, timesheets, invoices — at any time.

That's HourKeep's entire pricing philosophy: $19, $49 or $99 a month, unlimited everything, exports forever. The number on the pricing page is the number on your invoice. Boring, predictable, and — after a year of 16× surprises — exactly the point.
