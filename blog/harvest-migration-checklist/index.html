The afternoon Harvest migration checklist.

Guide · 5 min readBy the HourKeep team

Seven steps, one afternoon, zero lost billable hours.

Teams stay on overpriced tools for one reason: migration sounds like a project, and consultancies don't have spare projects. But a Harvest migration isn't a replatforming — it's a data move with a well-defined shape. Clients, projects, tasks, timesheets, invoices. Here's the checklist we walk every team through. Most finish in an afternoon.

1. Confirm your renewal date

Everything works backwards from this. Your legacy rate holds until the annual contract renews, so that date is your deadline — not the day the shocking invoice arrives. Put it on the calendar and give yourself at least two weeks of buffer.

2. Export everything while it's cheap

Before you touch anything else, pull a full export: clients, projects, tasks, users, timesheet history and invoices. Do it now, on the old terms, while exports are still free and complete. Store a copy somewhere you control. This archive is your insurance — even if the migration stalls, no one can hold your history hostage.

3. Audit seats vs actual humans

Open the team list and mark who logged hours in the last 60 days. Almost every consultancy finds ghosts: contractors who rolled off, hires who never started, part-timers with full seats. Your new plan should be sized to real humans. On HourKeep's $19 / $49 / $99 tiers, this audit alone often drops teams a full tier.

4. Map your structure, don't redesign it

Migration week is not the time to reorganise your chart of accounts. Mirror your existing client → project → task hierarchy exactly. Same names, same codes, same billing rates. You can tidy up later; right now, familiarity is what gets the team logging hours on day one without training.

5. Import history, then verify one invoice

Bring over clients, projects and timesheet history, then pick a single recent invoice and rebuild it in the new tool. If the hours, rates and totals match to the penny, your import is good — that's your proof the books survived the move. If they don't match, you've found the mapping error while Harvest is still there to compare against.

6. Run one week in parallel

Have the team log hours in both tools for a single week. Yes, it's mildly annoying for five days. It catches every workflow gap — the approval chain someone forgot, the report the partners actually read, the integration that matters — while the old system is still the safety net. Then cut over on a Monday, when timesheets are naturally empty.

7. Cancel, and keep the archive

Cancel Harvest before the renewal date, confirm the cancellation in writing, and keep your export archive somewhere permanent. A good tool lets you re-export forever — that's the test. If leaving is easy, staying is a choice, and choices keep vendors honest.
