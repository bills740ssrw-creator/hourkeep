Harvest's 16× price shock, explained.

Pricing · 6 min readBy the HourKeep team

The bills below were reported by the BBC in 2026 following Harvest's acquisition.

In 2026, long-time Harvest customers started opening renewal notices that didn't look like renewals at all. A UK consultancy went from $130 to $2,110 a month. A US customer saw an annual bill jump from $2,800 to $23,000. A solo user on an Unlimited plan was quoted $12 to $1,900 a month. The BBC covered the fallout — and if you run a consultancy or agency, the story matters whether or not you use Harvest.

What actually changed

Two things happened at once. First, Bending Spoons acquired Harvest, and acquisitions reprice — new owners inherit your data, your workflows and your switching costs, which makes it the perfect moment to change the deal. Second, the pricing model itself changed: flat per-seat pricing became per-seat plus unpublished usage fees. The seat price you signed up for stayed on the page. The total on the invoice became something else entirely.

That second part is the one to internalise. A per-seat price you can multiply is a budget. A per-seat price plus meters you can't see until the invoice lands is a lottery ticket — one your finance team loses.

Nobody knows their bill until the invoice lands. That sentence should never describe a tool you run your business on.

Why consultancies got hit hardest

Agencies are the worst possible customer for usage-based time tracking. Your usage isn't a steady line — it spikes with client work, which is exactly when you're too busy to audit a SaaS bill. More projects, more clients, more invoices: all of it reads as "usage" to the meter, even though none of it costs the vendor anything extra to serve. You do more business, your tooling bill multiplies, and your margins compress from both sides.

Your renewal date is your deadline

Here's the part most teams miss: legacy rates typically hold until the annual contract renews. That renewal date isn't just a bill — it's a window. Before it closes, you can export everything on the old terms and move at your own pace. After it closes, you're negotiating from inside the new pricing. So the first thing to do today is simple: find your Harvest renewal date and work backwards from it.

What to do this week

  1. Find your renewal date. Check your Harvest billing settings or your last invoice. That's your migration deadline.
  2. Export everything now, while it's cheap. Clients, projects, tasks, timesheet history — download it all. An export you already hold is leverage no acquisition can reprice.
  3. Count real seats, not licensed seats. Most teams pay for people who stopped logging hours months ago. Know your true headcount before you compare alternatives.
  4. Demand a number in writing. Any replacement tool should give you a flat monthly figure — no usage layers, no "contact sales" trap doors — plus a written grandfather clause.
  5. Move before the notice, not after it. Our migration checklist walks through the whole move in seven steps. Most teams finish in an afternoon.

The Harvest story isn't really about Harvest. It's about what happens when the tool that records your billable hours gets to decide what your hours are worth to them. Pick tools whose pricing you can do on a napkin — and keep your data exportable, so the next acquisition email is someone else's problem.
